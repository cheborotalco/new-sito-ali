01 — Approach

Successful projects are rarely about technology alone.

What decides the outcome is the quality of the decisions around it: the scope you accept, the risks you name early, and the people who own the result.

01 // EDUCATION

Technical roots, managerial practice

With a foundation in Computer Science and a Master’s degree in Project Management, I have continuously reinforced my expertise through multiple industry-recognized certifications. This background allows me to bridge technical execution with business strategy and speak the language of both engineers and executives.

02 // EXPERIENCE

Five years, end to end

For more than five years I have run IT and AI initiatives from concept and requirements through launch and continuous development — coordinating stakeholders, clients, and technical teams across Italy, Europe, and Asia. I have steered the delivery of complex digital products by negotiating with technical partners, optimizing operational costs, and translating high-level requirements into clear, sustainable technical roadmaps.

03 // FOCUS

Structure, then momentum

I create structure, bring order to complexity, and align people around the few priorities that actually matter. I translate strategic ambition into realistic execution cycles, setting unambiguous success metrics and ensuring every contributor understands their direct impact on the end result. My job is to make good decisions possible — and then to keep delivery honest.

02 — Focus areas

Where I am actually useful

Not a generic project-manager skill list. Four areas where I bring real depth: Agile delivery, AI fundamentals, cloud foundations, and governance that survives contact with reality.

01Agile & product

Agile delivery & product ownership

Scrum done properly, not Scrum theatre: clear accountabilities, a backlog that reflects value, and delivery you can forecast.

  • Scrum and Kanban — roles, events, artefacts, flow
  • Backlog management, refinement and prioritisation
  • Sprint goals, velocity and flow metrics that mean something
  • Working with Product Owners, POs and development teams
  • Hybrid models where the organisation is not ready for pure Agile
02AI foundations

AI fundamentals — beyond the buzzwords

I know what is inside the box: which model family fits which problem, what the data has to look like, and how to tell whether it works.

  • Learning paradigms: supervised, unsupervised, reinforcement learning
  • Model families: predictive ML, computer vision, NLP, recommendation
  • Foundation models and LLMs — capabilities and hard limits
  • Prompt design, fine-tuning vs RAG, embeddings and context
  • Evaluation, hallucination control, cost and latency trade-offs
03Cloud · AWS

Cloud foundations

Enough AWS fluency to talk architecture, cost and security with the technical team — and to know which questions to ask a vendor.

  • AWS core services: compute, storage, networking, databases
  • IAM, security and the shared responsibility model
  • Regions, availability and deployment models (cloud, hybrid)
  • Cost management, billing and architecture trade-offs
  • Migration and adoption patterns for traditional businesses
04Governance

Delivery governance, PMP-grade

The unglamorous part that decides whether a project lands: scope, schedule, budget, risk, and a single version of the truth.

  • Scope definition, WBS and critical path planning
  • Budget control, forecasting and earned value
  • Risk registers, assumptions and mitigation plans
  • Change control without bureaucracy for its own sake
  • Executive reporting and stakeholder alignment
